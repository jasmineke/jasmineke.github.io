Cart Editor Rebuilding cart from the ground up for agencies and premier merchants.

A working log, documented as the project happens.

June 17, 2026

Like with designs, I'm trying something new and starting to document as I work on the project. This way I don't need to retroactively recall the thoughts and details of a project, and it's a little experiment I can have with myself on what I think transpired in retrospect vs. what actually transpired — and how that experience felt.

So far we've handled many complex issues. The core clients for our product are premier merchants. But to truly service our premier merchants, we need to also service their agencies. Since many large Shopify merchants have their own agencies to configure their storefront and optimize their AOV, it becomes more and more important for us to make our cart more flexible for custom code (for agencies) and more flexible for the merchants who hire these agencies to drop in and make simple changes.

To deal with this, we needed to look at our cart editor today, and how competitors and similar "storefront editing" services were servicing these settings to merchants.

When we took a look at competitors, I gathered our top competitors as well as explored other editors that were tried and tested to dissect what made them simple to use at scale.

Our top competitors at the time were: Rebuy and Instant.

Whereas great editors that I've worked with and are tried and true were canvas editors like Figma, Framer, Webflow and, because our merchants are on Shopify, Shopify's theme editor.

But honestly, beyond looking at competitors, what was more pivotal in helping us move forwards was understanding the problems merchants had today with cart and what they really wanted.

Merchants often wanted custom components and in a specific way, so agencies needed a way to flexibly reorder, and add their own blocks without the restriction of settings.

Upcart today (previous to our rebuild) used modules that were fixed in place. Although we had the concept of "custom code," merchants couldn't inject custom code where they wanted and reorder it flexibly.

To solve for that — the first step was how we framed the cart to begin with. Instead of fixing the modules, what if we made them re-orderable?
